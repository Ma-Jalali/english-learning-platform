What an English-language test can measure

A language assessment gathers evidence rather than measuring a single, invisible quality. Reading tasks can show how a candidate identifies a writer’s claim, follows relationships between ideas, and interprets detail in context. Other tasks reveal related processes, while productive responses require candidates to construct meaning for another person.

Separate skill results therefore provide a profile. A candidate might follow a complex written argument with confidence but need more control when organising an extended spoken answer. The profile makes that difference visible and gives future study a clearer direction.

Instructions form part of the task. In completion questions, the phrase NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER limits the acceptable response. A candidate who identifies the right idea but ignores the limit has not met the complete task condition.

Careful candidates identify the source, response type and limit before searching. They then check grammar, spelling and fit after entering each response. This approach is more reliable than treating instructions as decoration.
