01

Orient

One test, four kinds of evidence.

IELTS does not measure one vague idea of “good English”. It gathers evidence from four skills: how you understand written and spoken language, and how clearly you communicate in writing and speech.

Academic and General Training candidates share the same Listening and Speaking tests. Reading and Writing differ because the contexts and communication purposes are different.

Why are results reported as skill bands?

A separate band for each skill reveals a profile rather than a single total. A learner may understand complex reading texts confidently while still needing more control in extended speaking.

02

Notice

What IELTS measures

Every task creates an opportunity to demonstrate a specific part of communicative ability. Knowing the purpose of a task helps you choose a strategy without trying to predict tricks.

Reading
Locate ideas, distinguish claims, follow relationships and interpret detail in context.
Listening
Follow purpose, detail, attitude and development across conversations and talks.
Writing
Address the task, organise ideas, use vocabulary precisely and control grammar.
Speaking
Develop ideas fluently, choose language flexibly and remain intelligible.
!

Exam tip · Instruction evidence

Treat instructions as part of the question.

“NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER” is a scoring condition. Check the word limit before listening or scanning, then check it again before moving on.
